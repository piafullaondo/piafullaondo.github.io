The Challenge that Moral Decisions Pose to Artificial Agency

Undergraduate (MA) Philosophy Dissertation (2024) - Pia Fullaondo

Moral Decisions and Artificial Moral Agents

Moral decisions present challenges to Artificial Moral Agents that undermine our motivations to pursue such entities.

Concerns Raised by the Delegation of Moral Decisions to AMAs

Evasion and Relocation of Moral Responsibility

The desire to delegate moral decision-making to AMAs reflects an inclination to evade the burden of moral responsibility.

Moral Deskilling and Desensitisation

The outsourcing of moral decision-making may lead to a decline in moral competencies and ethical awareness.

The Functional Moral Agent

The Gap in Moral Responsibility

Functional moral agents lack consciousness and free will, creating a gap in accountability.

The Degenerative Nature

Delegating moral decisions to such systems introduces risks without resolving core ethical challenges.

The Full Moral Agent

Moral Problems: Not an Issue of Quantity

The creation of full artificial agents is not justified by a shortage of moral decision-makers.

Futile Delegation

These agents would inherit similar limitations to human moral reasoning.

Moral Status and Slavery

Treating such agents as tools raises concerns about exploitation and moral status.

Conclusion

Artificial moral agents fail to offer a satisfactory solution to moral decision-making challenges.
